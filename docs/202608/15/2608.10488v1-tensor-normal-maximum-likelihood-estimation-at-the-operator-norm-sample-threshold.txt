1.
Introduction
For tensor-valued observations, an unrestricted covariance matrix has dimension D × D,
where D = Qk
a=1 da can be much larger than the sample size. The tensor-normal model
imposes the separable structure
Xi ∼N (0, Σ1 ⊗· · · ⊗Σk) ,
i = 1, . . . , n,
(1.1)
where Σa ∈PD(da). This structure makes likelihood inference possible in regimes in
which ordinary covariance estimation is hopeless. The maximum likelihood estimator
is attractive because it is equivariant, requires no tuning parameter and is computable
arXiv:2608.10488v1  [math.ST]  11 Aug 2026


by the classical flip-flop iteration (Dutilleul, 1999). Its finite-sample behavior is subtle,
however: the likelihood need not be bounded, the maximum need not exist or be unique,
and a naive Euclidean analysis introduces condition-number dependence.
The almost-sure existence and uniqueness problem has been studied through matrix
pencils, quiver representations and geometric invariant theory (Drton et al., 2021; Derksen
& Makam, 2021; Derksen et al., 2022). These works give exact, format-dependent algebraic
classifications, but do not provide nonasymptotic localization or estimation error bounds
for the MLE. A different line of work studies alternative estimators and asymptotic
geometry; see, for example, McCormack & Hoff (2025) and Drton et al. (2024).
The direct predecessor of the present paper is Franks et al. (2026). Using the affine-
invariant geometry of positive definite matrices and expansion of random completely
positive maps, they proved condition-number-free guarantees for the tensor-normal MLE.
If dmax = maxa da, their tensor result requires
nD ≳k2d3
max.
(1.2)
Under this condition the MLE exists uniquely and achieves nearly sharp Fisher–Rao error
rates. Their Section 6 identifies as the main open question whether (1.2) can be weakened
to
nD ≳k2d2
max.
(1.3)
The distinction is statistical rather than cosmetic. The cubic scale is the one at which
constant Frobenius error becomes possible for the largest factor, whereas the quadratic
scale is the natural threshold for constant operator-norm error. For a balanced third-order
tensor, the improvement allows one sample once the mode dimension is sufficiently large.
We prove that (1.3) suffices, while retaining the same Fisher–Rao error bounds. The
result is uniform over the true Kronecker factors and assumes neither bounded condition
number nor sparsity. For k = 2, it also yields the natural threshold n ≳dmax/dmin without
the logarithmic factors in the earlier matrix-normal sufficient condition; Corollary 1 also
gives factorwise Thompson-metric guarantees without those logarithmic factors. Our
main motivation is the open problem for k ≥3.
The proof uses the random-matrix estimate of Franks et al. (2026), itself based on work
of Pisier (2012, 2014), but the localization argument is different from scaling-flow analyses.
The recent ∞-expansion method of Lau & Ramachandran (2025) obtains optimal bounds
for Tyler’s M-estimator and points to tensor-normal estimation as a possible application.
A direct use of that framework would require uniform control of operator-norm errors
along a scaling trajectory. Such a uniform contraction is unavailable for the pairwise
completely positive maps arising here. To see the obstruction, even if Φ is balanced and has
Kraus rank N, then for a unit vector u the matrix Φ∗(uuT ) is supported on a subspace of
dimension at most N; a traceless matrix that is nearly constant on this support witnesses
an ℓ∞contraction coefficient tending to one when N/d →0. An ∞-expansion approach
would therefore have to exploit the particular data-dependent trajectory rather than
arbitrary operator-norm directions. Our proof avoids a scaling-flow argument: it uses
two-sided Hilbert-space control of the likelihood and concentration of the constrained,
data-dependent optimizer.
There is also a substantial literature on robust covariance estimation under structural
constraints. Convexly constrained and relaxed versions of Tyler’s estimator were stud-
ied by Soloveychik & Wiesel (2014), while Sun et al. (2016) treated general structural
constraints, including a Kronecker model, through majorization–minimization. Those


works target elliptical or heavy-tailed distributions and emphasize robustness and compu-
tation. In contrast, our theorem concerns the unregularized Gaussian Kronecker MLE
and gives condition-number-free, nonasymptotic localization at a dimension-dependent
sample threshold. Spherical Tyler samples retain orthogonal invariance, but the Euclidean
Gaussian Lipschitz concentration used after our equivariant-centering step does not ap-
ply verbatim to normalized heavy-tailed observations. The localization principle could
nevertheless extend to other smooth Gaussian models generated by group actions; a
robust extension would additionally require an analogue of the full-Lie Gram bound and
suitable control of the loss sensitivity together with a replacement for Gaussian Lipschitz
concentration.
1.1.
Contributions and proof strategy
The proof has four ingredients.
First, Theorem C.1 in the supplementary material to Franks et al. (2026) is stated on
the entire traceless matrix space, even though their final Hessian statement is restricted
to symmetric tangents. Retaining this stronger event gives a well-conditioned Gram form
on the full local Lie algebra
R ⊕
k
M
a=1
Mat0(da; R).
Second, a statistical tangent at a nonidentity point becomes nonsymmetric after conjuga-
tion. The full-Lie bound therefore transports exactly to a fixed Thompson ball, with only
a constant factor loss and no dependence on k or the dimensions.
Third, we minimize the likelihood over that fixed ball. Uniform lower and upper Hessian
bounds imply that this constrained optimizer is O((nD)−1/2)-Lipschitz as a function
of the stacked data. Fourth, the logarithm of each determinant-normalized factor is a
Hilbert-valued Lipschitz map on the good data set. Kirszbraun extension followed by
averaging over the local orthogonal group produces a globally defined, exactly centered
equivariant map. Gaussian concentration and a sphere net then localize every factor
directly in operator norm. The constrained optimizer lies strictly inside the ball and hence
equals the unconstrained MLE.
The information-theoretic comparison is also clean. If all nuisance factors are known,
estimating a factor of dimension da reduces to ordinary Gaussian precision estimation
from nD/da samples. Thus constant operator accuracy for the largest factor requires
nD = Ω(d2
max). Our threshold is consequently optimal in its dependence on dmax for fixed
k. We do not claim optimal dependence on k, an exact algebraic existence threshold, or
flip-flop convergence at the improved sample size.

9.
Discussion
Theorem 1 resolves the explicit question posed in Section 6 of Franks et al. (2026): the
guarantees of their tensor-normal MLE theorem continue to hold when d3
max in the sample
threshold is replaced by d2
max. The proof does not require a new random-channel inequality.
Instead, it retains the full-matrix form of an existing random Gram event and combines
it with a geometric and probabilistic localization principle. The main conceptual lesson is
that coercivity on the full orbit Lie algebra can be transported throughout a condition-
number bounded region, while equivariance can turn Hilbert-valued sensitivity of an
optimizer into operator-norm concentration without a Frobenius-to-operator conversion.
Several questions remain. First, the exact existence and uniqueness boundary is format
dependent and is described algebraically by Derksen et al. (2022); the present uniform
sufficient condition is not intended to replace that classification. Second, the optimal
dependence on k is unknown. Third, the argument establishes statistical properties
of the MLE but does not prove linear convergence of the flip-flop algorithm at the
improved sample threshold. Finally, it would be useful to isolate an abstract version of
the constrained-optimizer localization argument for other Gaussian models generated by
reductive group actions.
